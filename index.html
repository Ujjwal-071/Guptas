Rulers of the Gupta Period

Textbook pages 67-68
Your note from the book: learn the rulers only in brief.

The Guptas were a royal family of ancient India. The first two kings, Sri Gupta and Ghatotkacha, are not well known. So we say the Gupta Era began with Chandragupta I. Learn three big kings: Chandragupta I, Samudragupta and Chandragupta II.

Who came after whom?

Easy trickFirst letters are C, S, C, K, S. Say: "Can Some Clever Kids Study?"

Meet the kings (tap to open)

1Chandragupta I
  • The first independent king of the Guptas.
  • He took the title Maharajadhiraja, which means "king of kings".
  • He declared his freedom in Magadha.
  • He married Kumaradevi, a princess of the Lichchhavi family. This made his empire bigger.
  • His gold coins record this marriage.
2Samudragupta
  • Son of Chandragupta I. A great conqueror, a clever leader and a friend of the arts.
  • Called the 'Napoleon of India' because he was brave and a great general.
  • In his famous Deccan campaign he travelled more than 3,000 miles through thick forests.
  • He did not take over the south. Southern kings accepted his power and kept ruling.
  • He worshipped Lord Vishnu but respected other religions.
  • He played the veena. One of his coins shows it. He made 8 types of gold coins.
  • He did an Ashvamedha Yajna (horse sacrifice).
3Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)
  • Son of Samudragupta. He was a strong fighter like his father.
  • He took Malwa, Gujarat and Saurashtra from the Sakas. So he got the title Sakari.
  • Now his empire touched the western-coast ports. Ships could carry goods to Europe through Egypt.
  • He married Kuberanaga of the powerful Naga family. This made him stronger.
4Kumaragupta and Skandagupta

These two kings come next. Kumaragupta I started Nalanda University (see Lesson 2).

The Allahabad Pillar

  • Poet Harisena wrote it to praise Samudragupta.
  • It is also called Prayaga Prashasti.
  • It lists the kings and tribes he won over.
  • It shows us the map of India in the first half of the 4th century CE.

Education and Nalanda

Textbook pages 71-72

In Gupta times, learned teachers taught students in schools and monasteries. The most famous university was Nalanda. It was very hard to get in, and students came from many countries.

Teachers and students

  • Teachers were learned Brahmanas called Acharyas and Upadhyayas. Some were called Bhattas.
  • Kings gave them villages called agraharas. The public also gave gifts.
  • Students were called shishyas.
  • They studied 14 subjects (chaturdasavidya): 4 Vedas, 6 Vedangas, Puranas, Mimansa, Nyaya, Dharma, grammar, and the Mahabharata.

Buddhist monasteries

  • Students stayed for 10 years.
  • First they learned by listening and speaking. Later they read books.
  • Monasteries had libraries. Important manuscripts were copied and kept safe.
  • Kings and rich traders paid for the monasteries.
  • Chinese traveller Fa-hien studied for years in a monastery at Pataliputra.
Two famous universities: Takshila (started before the Guptas) and Nalanda.

Nalanda University

Your notebook hints (page 71):
  • 1. Kumaragupta I
  • 2. Balaputradeva
  • 3. RRR (3 libraries)
  • 4. 8 colleges
  • 5. Entry test: 20% pass
  • 6. 10,000+ students
  • Most famous school of ancient India. It was at Nalanda, near Rajagriha in Bihar.
  • Started by emperor Kumaragupta I. Later, the Pala kings helped it.
  • We know about it from Hiuen Tsang, a Chinese Buddhist scholar who visited India in the time of Harshavardhana.
  • At least 8 colleges. Balaputradeva, King of Sumatra, built one. A brick wall went around the whole campus.
  • 3 libraries: Ratnasagar, Ratnodadhi and Ratnaranjak. (Trick: RRR)
  • Subjects: Vedas, logic, grammar, medicine, sankhya, yoga, nyaya and Buddhist books.
  • It was a post-graduate university. Only the most brilliant students got in. Hardly 20 out of 100 passed the entry test.
  • 10,000+ students and teachers. Famous teachers: Aryabhatta, Harsha, Dharmapala, Nagarjuna, Dharmakirti, Asanga, Vasubandhu, Chandrakirti and Silabhadra.
  • Hiuen Tsang studied there for 5 years in the 7th century.
  • Students came from China, Japan, Korea, Tibet, Mongolia, Sri Lanka and South-East Asia.
  • It lasted 8 centuries. Invaders destroyed it in the 12th century. It became a UN Heritage Site in 2016.

Science

Textbook page 72

The Gupta age grew fast in astronomy, maths, medicine and metallurgy. The Iron Pillar that never rusts is the best proof.

Famous scholars

ScholarKnown for
VarahamihiraAstronomer. He said the moon goes round the earth and the earth goes round the sun. His book Brihat Samhita is about astronomy, plants, nature and geography.
BrahmaguptaMathematician and astronomer. He described the law of gravitation centuries before Newton.
Charaka, SushruthaFamous scholars of Ayurveda.

Metals and art

  • Metals were studied very well.
  • Proof: the Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, near Qutub Minar, New Delhi. It has not rusted after so many centuries.
  • Many bronze statues of Buddha were made.
  • The Ajanta paintings show that making colours was advanced.

Aryabhatta

A great astronomer and mathematician. His two books are Aryabhattiyam and Surya Siddhanta.

Your note in the book: write any 4 achievements.
  1. He said the earth moves round the sun, many centuries before the Europeans.
  2. He found the rule for the area of a triangle.
  3. He gave the value of pi as 3.1416. The credit for trigonometry should go to him.
  4. In Surya Siddhanta he explained the causes of solar and lunar eclipses.
  5. He knew the decimal system and the use of zero.
Easy trickRemember 5 words: Sun, Triangle, Pi, Eclipse, Zero.

Medicine

Textbook pages 72-73

Doctors in the Gupta age were great at Ayurveda, preventing illness and surgery. The most famous surgeon was Sushrutha.

The big ideas

  • Doctors built on two older books: the Charaka Samhita and the Sushrutha Samhita.
  • Charaka's book teaches holistic healthcare: both curing and preventing illness.
  • People tried to prevent disease with good food, a healthy life and even early vaccination.

Sushrutha: the numbers

600 BCEAbout when he lived
1,120Diseases he described
101 + 20Blunt + sharp instruments he made
8Main types of surgery he taught
  • Doctor and teacher, called the 'Father of Plastic Surgery'.
  • He did nose reconstruction using skin from the cheek.
  • His Sushrutha Samhita is the oldest surgical text.
  • His ways came before Hippocrates and Galen. He even used household things, which shows how clever he was.
Your note in the book: write any 4 to 5 achievements.
  1. He set the code of medical ethics.
  2. He said doctors must do a full physical check-up.
  3. He described 1,120 diseases. He was the first to link malaria with mosquitoes and plague with rats.
  4. He made early diagnosis of diabetes possible.
  5. He made 101 blunt and 20 sharp instruments, and said the edge must be very fine.
  6. He taught 8 types of surgery.

The 8 types of surgery:

  • Extraction
  • Excision
  • Incision
  • Probing
  • Scarifying
  • Suturing
  • Puncturing
  • Fluid evacuation
Easy trickMalaria = Mosquito. Plague = Rat. Both start with the same letter.

Culture and Temples

Textbook page 73

The Gupta kings kept the country peaceful, so trade grew and people became rich. Rich people could enjoy arts, crafts and books. Beautiful temples were built too.

Why did culture grow?

  • Strong kings gave peace (political stability).
  • Peace meant more trade. Trade meant more money.
  • With money, people enjoyed arts, crafts and writing.

Old temples vs Gupta temples

  • Before the Guptas: temples were made of wood, which is perishable.
  • Gupta temples: made of bricks and stone.
  • Style: Nagara.
  • Two famous ones in Uttar Pradesh: Deogarh (near Jhansi) is made of stone. Bhitargaon (near Kanpur) is made of bricks.
Easy trickB for Bhitargaon = B for Bricks. So Deogarh is the stone one.

Vishnu (Dashavatara) Temple, Deogarh

  • About 125 km from Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh.
  • One of the earliest Hindu stone temples still standing.
  • The first north Indian temple with a Shikhara (tower). A part of it has fallen.
  • Built on a 1.5 metre high platform. Four stairways lead up to it.
  • The outside shows scenes from the Ramayana.
  • Over the front door: Vishnu with his avatars Narasimha and Vamana.
  • The idol inside is missing.
  • Local name: Sagar marh, meaning "temple on the tank", because there is a square water pool cut into the rock in front.

Three side niches

Sheshnaga
Vishnu sleeping on the coil of the giant serpent.
Nar Narayana
The back niche. God and human joined together.
Gajendra moksha
Vishnu comes to save the trapped elephant, Gaja.

Books and Kalidasa

Textbook page 74

In the Gupta age, Sanskrit became the language of kings and scholars. Many books were written. The greatest writer was Kalidasa.

Language and books

  • Sanskrit replaced Prakrit in inscriptions, religion and philosophy. It stayed the scholars' language for about 1,000 years.
  • The Guptas made Sanskrit the official language.
  • People wrote epics, poems, plays, songs and prose.
  • The Puranas began to be written: Vishnu, Vayu, Varaha, Narsimha, Vaman, Shiva and Matsya Purana. They tell stories of gods.
  • Some Smritis (law books) were written too.

Famous writers:

  • Kalidasa
  • Sudraka
  • Bhasa
  • Harisena
  • Dandin
  • Subandhu
  • Bharavi

Kalidasa

  • The greatest poet and playwright India has produced.
  • Vincent Smith compared the Gupta Age to England's Elizabethan period. Kalidasa shines like Shakespeare.
  • His plays are translated into many world languages.
4 poems
  • Ritusamhara
  • Raghuvamsa
  • Meghaduta (The Cloud Messenger)
  • Kumarasambhava
3 plays
  • Vikramorvashiyam
  • Malavikagnimitram
  • Abhijnanashakuntalam
Easy trick4 poems + 3 plays = 7 works. Say them in order: "R, R, M, K" for the poems.
  • Abhijnanashakuntalam is the love story of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala. Their son Bharata later became king.
  • In his plays, high-caste characters speak Sanskrit. Lower castes and women speak Prakrit. This shows the status of women went down in this time.
  • His works are about love, nature and beauty, but they also tell us about the government, society and religion of the Gupta period.
